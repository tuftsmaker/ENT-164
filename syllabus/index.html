Overview

How can we harness maker tools and technology to build working prototypes that address real-world challenges?

This entrepreneurial course demystifies technology by teaching you to build smart device prototypes using physical making, basic electronics, and programming. By creating working prototypes, you'll master the iterative innovation process essential to entrepreneurship: build, test, learn, and improve. This practical approach transforms technology from an intimidating black box into an accessible tool for bringing your ideas to life. You'll develop an entrepreneurial mindset by learning to work as a team, fail fast, and iterate rapidly.

This class introduces basic programming techniques to make devices interact with their environment. Prior programming experience is not a requirement — students will learn how to leverage AI to program microcontrollers, so a general interest in programming is suggested.

No restrictions on enrollment. All undergraduate and graduate students are welcome, including first-year undergrads. No prior experience in design thinking, innovation, or programming is needed.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

Make & fabricate

  • Design and fabricate physical objects using CAD, laser cutters, and 3D printers
  • Build functional circuits and program microcontrollers using MicroPython and AI

Connect & integrate

  • Create smart IoT devices that sense, visualize, and respond to environmental data
  • Integrate multiple technologies into a cohesive working prototype

Collaborate & iterate

  • Work effectively in multidisciplinary teams to design and build prototypes
  • Apply the "build-measure-learn" loop through rapid iteration and failing fast

Communicate & present

  • Present working prototypes to peers and public audiences
  • Articulate design decisions and lessons learned

How class works

This course is intended to be taught in person. We expect students to make every effort to join us in the (physical) classroom unless they encounter extenuating circumstances. Important maker, prototyping, and programming skills will be demonstrated in class — if you need to miss class, you will need to make an appointment with an instructor or a TA to help you catch up.

For most weeks, class time is divided into two parts. We meet in the classroom for the first 60–90 minutes to recap the previous class, introduce foundational maker skills, and complete short exercises. We then move to Nolop for the second half to apply those skills and work on team projects.

Weekly reflections

Reflection is a critical part of the learning process. At the end of each class, you'll take a few minutes to reflect on what you learned, what challenged you, and how you might apply new skills to your projects. Submit your weekly reflection within 24 hours of each class — reflections are part of your participation grade.

Course modules

The course is divided into two modules.

Module 1 · Foundational skills

Learn to make physical things using hand making techniques, CAD tools, 3D printers, and laser cutting. You'll work on basic electronics projects to enable your projects to interact with the world. In Week 2, you'll be placed into teams of 4–5 students and collaborate on the Robo Challenge: design and build a robot that can move as many balls as possible between two lines. You'll also start defining the final project you'll present on demo day.

Module 2 · Final project work

Build on the first module's foundation to develop your team's final project. You'll learn advanced skills including data sensing, visualization, and AI integration. Your team chooses the real-world challenge you want to tackle. Your final project must demonstrate the core maker techniques: laser cutting, 3D printing, electronics, connectivity, and AI. The module ends with a public demo day.

Grading

How your grade is weighted, and the policies around assignments.

  • Individual assignments 35%
  • Team assignments, presentations, and reports (team grades are shared by default but may be adjusted based on individual performance) 35%
  • Peer and self evaluations (mid-term and final) 20%
  • Reflections and participation 10%
Peer and self evaluations. Twice during the semester (mid-term and final), you'll assess your own contributions and those of your teammates. These evaluations help ensure fair grading for team projects and encourage accountability within your team. Your feedback is confidential and will be used to adjust individual grades when contributions are unequal.

Assignment policies

  • All assignments are submitted through Canvas.
  • Assignments are typically due on Tuesdays by noon the day of class.
  • There will be a 25% late fee for submissions received during the grace period (within 1 week of the deadline). We will not accept submissions beyond the grace period.
  • Missed work due to illness: if you are sick and unable to complete an assignment on time, contact the instructor before the deadline to discuss an extension.
  • Missed presentations: if you are too ill to present in class, we can arrange for you to present remotely or reschedule.

Course materials

All materials are provided by the course at no cost to students. You will receive an electronics starter kit at the beginning of the semester. Additional materials and tools will be available at Nolop. No textbooks are required.

What to bring to class

  • Laptop (required)

Online platforms and tools

Norms & policies

Attendance policy

  • On-time, in-person class attendance and respectful class participation is required.
  • If you need to miss class, arrive late, leave early (e.g., to attend a job interview), or attend remotely due to extenuating circumstances (e.g., illness, travel overseas for family reasons), notify instructors by 5:30pm the day before class. You may also use the Short-Term Illness Notification tool in SIS.
  • Absences due to illness: if you are too ill to attend class, inform the instructor as soon as possible. You do not need to provide medical documentation. Please do not come to class if you are sick; we will work with you to catch up on missed material.
  • When doing group work, observe team process norms including equal participation of all team members. These norms will be introduced at the beginning of the semester.
  • Students are expected to adhere to the school's honor code.
  • Diversity is celebrated at Tufts, as it should be around the world. Diversity is multidimensional and spans gender identity, sexual orientation, race, prior experiences, and more. Respectful, inclusive, and equitable treatment of all students and instructors is a fundamental norm in this class.

Academic integrity

Tufts holds its students strictly accountable for adherence to academic integrity. The consequences for violations can be severe. It is critical that you understand the requirements of ethical behavior and academic work as described in Tufts' Academic Misconduct policy. If you ever have a question about the expectations concerning a particular assignment or project in this course, be sure to ask one of the instructors for clarification.

While collaboration is encouraged, individual assignments are expected to be submitted individually using the components provided to the student. Allocate sufficient time to request replacement components if required — it is your responsibility to pick up new components promptly when missing a class. Submitting an individual assignment as shared work will result in points being divided among the participants.

On the use of AI

This course integrates AI as a core tool in the maker's workflow. You will learn to use AI to design, iterate, and build more advanced prototypes than would otherwise be possible. AI use is encouraged and expected. You may use tools like Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, and other AI assistants for:

  • Generating and debugging code for microcontrollers
  • Brainstorming design ideas
  • Understanding technical concepts
  • Troubleshooting electronics and fabrication issues

Attribution is not required, but documenting your AI-assisted workflow is encouraged as a learning practice.
