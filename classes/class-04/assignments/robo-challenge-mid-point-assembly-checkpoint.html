This checkpoint verifies that your team has a physically assembled, structurally sound robot (and dispenser if applicable) with all electronics mounted before you begin writing software. The goal is to catch mechanical and integration problems now, while there is still time to fix them. At this stage you do not yet need to wire all components together but you should have a plan for routing the wires.

Your robot does not need to move, sense, or perform its challenge task yet. It needs to be built well enough that software will work when you add it.

What to demonstrate at Nolop:

  • Your fully assembled robot and one supporting dispenser (if applicable)

What to submit in Canvas:

  • At least 3 photos of the build from different angles.
  • Your team’s 1-page self-assessment (see below)

Assembly Criteria:

Review the criterion below to understand what “good” looks like for the final challenge. Items highlighted in yellow should be the priority for the mid-point review:

Criterion

What We’re Looking For

Structural Integrity

Chassis is rigid and square. Joints are secure (screwed, glued, or press-fit as appropriate). No wobble or flex that would affect performance under motion.

Drivetrain & Mobility

Motors firmly mounted with no slop or misalignment. Wheels/treads attached and making consistent surface contact. Gears or belts (if used) are tensioned and aligned.

Electronics Mounting 

Microcontroller, motor driver, and sensors are fastened to the chassis - not dangling or taped on. Components positioned so they won’t interfere with moving parts.

Wiring & Connections

Wires connected to correct pins and seated firmly. Appropriate methods used (solder, screw terminals, or secure headers). No bare wire exposed where it could short. Wires routed away from wheels, gears, and heat sources.

Wire Management

Cabling is bundled or routed neatly and secured with zip ties or clips so nothing will snag or disconnect during movement.

Power System

Battery/power supply mounted securely, cannot shift during motion. Power and ground connections correct and protected. Battery accessible for charging or swapping without disassembly.

Sensor Placement

Sensors mounted in functional positions for the task (e.g., line sensor near surface, ultrasonic sensor at correct height). Mounting is secure and adjustable if needed.

Serviceability

Key components (battery, microcontroller, sensors) are accessible. A failed motor or sensor can be replaced without rebuilding the robot.

Design Intent

Component placement, weight distribution, and form factor show evidence of deliberate strategy for the challenge. Not only parts attached wherever they fit.

Common Pitfalls

Here's list of issues that come up every semester. Check these before your review:
•    Hot glue as a structural joint: It fails under vibration. Use mechanical fasteners for load-bearing connections.
•    Tape-mounted electronics: Masking tape will release mid-run. Use standoffs, screws, or adhesive-backed Velcro.
•    Wires routed through the drivetrain: A cable touching a spinning wheel will eventually get caught and ripped out.
•    No slack in sensor wires: If a sensor moves, its wires need strain relief and enough length to handle that motion.
•    Inaccessible battery: If swapping a battery requires partial disassembly, you’ll waste valuable testing time later.

Team Self-Assessment

Each team submits one short written self-assessment. This is not a formal report but an honest snapshot of where you are. Address:

  1. Current State: What is physically complete and wired? Be specific.
  2. Known Issues: What problems have you identified? What remains unresolved?
  3. Plan Forward: What will you prioritize in the remaining build time before software begins?

 

A note on this checkpoint

The purpose here is progress, not perfection. A system with a solid frame, clean wiring, and a couple of known issues you’ve written down is in great shape. A “finished” robot held together with tape and optimism is not. Show us where you are and that you have a clear-eyed plan for where you’re going.
