Learning outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

01

Hands-on making

Harness technology through hands-on making — no fear, and no prior physical making or coding knowledge required.

02

Maker tools proficiency

Develop confidence in concept drawing, hand modeling, 3D printing, laser cutting, electronics, and connected devices.

03

Team collaboration

Know how to collaborate in multidisciplinary teams to design and build working prototypes.

04

Entrepreneurial mindset

Apply the “build–measure–learn” loop through failing fast and doing rapid iterations.

Who should take this class?

If you see yourself here, you belong.

Aspiring entrepreneurs

Students looking to launch or work in early-stage tech ventures who want to build proof of concepts to vet ideas with customers.

Future inventors

Those interested in exploring ideas, understanding how things work under the covers, and experimenting with new technologies.

Hands-on team players

Individuals eager to collaborate in cross-functional teams and build real prototypes to test ideas rapidly.

Problem solvers & innovators

Students drawn to building tangible products to prove early concepts and gain traction for their ideas.

How class works

Most weeks follow the same rhythm: learn in the classroom, then build at Nolop.

Part 1

60–90 minutes

In the classroom

  • Last class recap
  • Foundational maker skills
  • Short exercises

Break

10–15 minutes

Walk to Nolop

  • Grab a snack
  • Head to the makerspace together

Part 2

60–90 minutes

In the makerspace

  • Apply maker skills
  • Team project work
Assignments. Individual assignments are small things you make on your own. Team assignments are the Robot Challenge in the first half of the semester and the Final Project in the second half.

Attendance & participation

Expectations

  • Actively participate in class activities and teamwork
  • Email me before class if you have a valid reason for missing
  • Inform your teammates if you can't make a team meeting
  • Team participation is evaluated via peer reviews

Key messages

  • This class simulates a real-world work environment
  • Don't hesitate to ask for help if you fall behind
  • Teamwork can be challenging — you're here to learn how to work effectively as a team

Grading

How your grade adds up.

  • Individual assignments 35%
  • Team assignments Robot Challenge and final project 35%
  • Peer evaluations mid-term and final 20%
  • Reflections and participation 10%
Team grades are shared by default, but may be adjusted based on individual performance.

Milestone reflections

Reflection is the engine of learning in project-based work.

What is it? A structured pause to examine your work, decisions, and learning process — turning experience into insight. Why it matters: without reflection, we repeat mistakes and miss growth opportunities.
1

Experience

Do the work

2

Reflect

Analyze what happened

3

Conceptualize

Extract insights

4

Apply

Improve next iteration

You'll do this five times this semester, at the milestones that close out each phase of the course: after 3D printing (Week 4), after connectivity (Week 6), after the Robo Challenge (Week 8), at the mid-point of the final project (Week 11), and a final reflection on the whole course (Week 13).

Key reflection questions

  • What worked well and why?
  • What would I do differently?
  • What did I learn about myself?
