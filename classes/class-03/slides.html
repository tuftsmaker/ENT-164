Numbers, not guesses · 1 of 2

Leverage datasheets

Every part you bought came with a drawing. Use it — the manufacturer already measured the part for you.

The four numbers you need

  • Mounting hole distance — 17.5 mm and 22.3 mm on the TT motor
  • Mounting hole diameter — Ø2.4 / Ø3 mm, depending on the screw
  • Motor housing height — 64.4 mm overall, 36.8 mm to the shaft
  • Motor housing depth — 22.6 mm, plus the 9.1 mm shaft

Check the drawing for hole spacing before you draw the mount — that single number decides whether the motor fits.

Numbers, not guesses · 2 of 2

Take your own measurements

When there's no datasheet, or the part is already in your hand, the caliper is the answer.

Outer jaws (1)

Measure the outside of a shaft, a wheel hub, or sheet thickness.

Inner jaws (2)

Measure the inside of a hole or a slot — the two small jaws at the top.

Depth rod (3)

Reach into a bore to find how deep a part really sits — handy for the motor boss.

Tip

Measure twice, and write the number on the sketch before you open Onshape.

Holding it together · 1 of 2

Bolts, washers, and nuts

Flat parts become a 3D object when you bolt them together — so your drawing needs the hole, not just the shape.

The bolt sizes the hole

An M3 bolt needs a clearance hole around 3.2 mm in the parts it passes through — the head and the nut do the clamping.

The washer spreads the load

Under the nut and the head, it stops the bolt from crushing soft plywood or cracking acrylic.

The nut locks it

Threads only bite where there is a matching nut, so plan for access on both sides of the joint.

ENT-164 · Intro to MakingClass 3 · Laser Cutting11

Holding it together · 2 of 2

Screws that make their own hole

Two screw types you'll use on wood and acrylic — and the pilot hole each one needs.

01

Self-tapping

Needs a pre-drilled pilot hole. The screw cuts its own threads as it goes in — a pilot hole slightly smaller than the screw keeps the wood from splitting.

Use it when the screw has to grip the material directly, with no nut on the far side.

02

Self-drilling

Has a drill point on the tip. It drills its own hole and taps its own threads in one pass — no pilot hole needed.

Fast for thin sheet and soft material, but it can split thin plywood or crack acrylic.

On the laser

You can't drill a hole with a laser — laser-cut a pilot hole slightly undersized and let the screw cut its own thread, or cut the clearance hole and use a bolt, washer, and nut.

Laser cutting vs 3D printing · 1 of 2

Two machines, two ways of making

Both are digital fabrication — but they remove material differently, so they suit different parts.

Aspect
3D printing
Laser cutting
Process
Additive — builds the object layer by layer
Subtractive — removes material from a flat sheet
Material
Thermoplastics, resins, and metal powders
Wood, acrylic, paper, fabric, and some plastics
Complexity
Intricate, organic 3D geometry
Best for 2D — 3D shapes are assembled from flat parts
Precision
Good resolution; layer lines affect the finish
High precision and tight tolerances on flat stock

Laser cutting vs 3D printing · 2 of 2

Choosing the right machine

Speed, cost, and finishing decide it — and often the answer is to use both.

Aspect
3D printing
Laser cutting
Speed
Slower on large or highly detailed objects
Fast from sheet stock — excellent for batch runs
Cost
Cost-effective for one-off prototypes and custom parts
Lower cost per part from standard sheet sizes
Finishing
Supports to remove, then sanding or finishing
Minimal — usually just cleaning the edges
Best for
Rapid prototypes and complex 3D models
Signage, panels, precision parts, high-volume cutting

At Nolop · laser cutting step by step 1–3

Open it in Inkscape and fix the lines

Your DXF will look wrong when it opens — that's normal. Three steps to make it cuttable.

The rule

Cuts are 100% red. Scores are 100% blue. Engraves and text are 100% black. The laser reads colour, so anything in the wrong colour is the wrong operation.
