Sensing and Visualization

A sensor reading is a number. A good visualization is an answer. Today we collect data from the physical world and put it on a screen in a form someone can act on.

Collecting data

You already have the parts: the same sensors and microcontrollers from Class 5 and Class 6.

What you can sense

  • Distance — ultrasonic and time-of-flight
  • Light, temperature, and humidity
  • Motion, tilt, and orientation
  • Sound level, or a button and a knob

Getting the reading out

  • Print it to the serial console
  • Serve it from the device's own page
  • Send it to a cloud service

Start small

Read one sensor reliably before you add a second. Two trustworthy numbers beat five flaky ones.

AI as a coding partner: describe the sensor and the reading you want, and let your AI assistant write the MicroPython. You tested this loop in Class 5 — keep it to one conversation.

Showing it clearly

The data is only useful if a person can read it at a glance. Three moves do most of the work.

Make 1

Pick the form

  • A live number when the value is what matters
  • A gauge or bar when it has a good range
  • A line over time when the trend matters

Make 2

Give it scale

  • Label the units
  • Show the range the value can take
  • Mark what counts as normal

Make 3

Make it live

  • Refresh on the device's own page
  • Colour for state — safe, warning, alarm
  • Keep it readable across the room
The test: hand your screen to someone who has never seen the project. If they can tell you what the number means and whether it is good or bad without asking, the visualization works.
