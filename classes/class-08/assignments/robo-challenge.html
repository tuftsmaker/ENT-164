The Goal

Design, build, and program a robot that moves as many balls as possible across the finish line within 5 minutes. 

Your robot must be fully designed and constructed by your team, integrating creativity, mechanical design, coding, and rapid prototyping.

This challenge is not about winning. It’s about:

  • Experimenting with ideas quickly

  • Building skills in sketching, prototyping, electronics, and coding

  • Collaborating effectively as a team

  • Discovering creative solutions to open-ended problems

Deliverables

  1. Working Robot and dispenser/receptacle (if applicable) – Ready for live competition and demonstration.

    • Live Demonstration – In-class attempt of the 5-minute Robo Challenge.

  2. Final Team Retrospective (1–2 pages) – Refer to the class's Team Retrospective Guide to reflect on:

    • What worked well and why

    • Obstacles, failures, and pivots along the way

    • Lessons learned for future projects

    • Action Items

  3. Photos of your Robot and Dispensers/Collectors.

Evaluation Criteria (100 points total)

1. Design and Implementation (75 pts)

Criterion

What We’re Looking For

Structural Integrity

Chassis is rigid and square. Joints are secure (screwed, glued, or press-fit as appropriate). No wobble or flex that would affect performance under motion.

Drivetrain & Mobility

Motors firmly mounted with no slop or misalignment. Wheels/treads attached and making consistent surface contact. Gears or belts (if used) are tensioned and aligned.

Electronics Mounting 

Microcontroller, motor driver, and sensors are fastened to the chassis - not dangling or taped on. Components positioned so they won’t interfere with moving parts.

Wiring & Connections

Wires connected to correct pins and seated firmly. Appropriate methods used (solder, screw terminals, or secure headers). No bare wire exposed where it could short. Wires routed away from wheels, gears, and heat sources.

Wire Management

Cabling is bundled or routed neatly and secured with zip ties or clips so nothing will snag or disconnect during movement.

Power System

Battery/power supply mounted securely, cannot shift during motion. Power and ground connections correct and protected. Battery accessible for charging or swapping without disassembly.

Sensor Placement

Sensors mounted in functional positions for the task (e.g., line sensor near surface, ultrasonic sensor at correct height). Mounting is secure and adjustable if needed.

Serviceability

Key components (battery, microcontroller, sensors) are accessible. A failed motor or sensor can be replaced without rebuilding the robot.

Design Intent

Component placement, weight distribution, and form factor show evidence of deliberate strategy for the challenge—not just parts attached wherever they fit.
