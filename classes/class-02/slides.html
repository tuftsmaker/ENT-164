The rules

How the race works

Read these twice — every design decision follows from them.

Scoring

Every ball counts

  • 1 point per ball moved across the line
  • Maximum 10 balls per trip
  • Balls are dropped off in a standard container

The course

5 minutes, 10 feet

  • Move as many balls as possible in 5 minutes
  • Loading and drop-off zones are 10 feet (~3 meters) apart

Your robot

Hands off during the race

  • Designed, built, and programmed by your team
  • You cannot touch the balls or robot during the race
  • Robot and loading mechanism must fit in your team's project box

How making actually works

Product development is not linear

Expect to loop — testing sends you back to the sketch, again and again.

The loop

Sketch → mockup → design → fabricate → assemble → program → test. Then test again.

Going backwards is progress

When a test fails, you go back a step — or all the way back to the sketch. That's how prototypes get better.

Build notes

Component details & wiring

During early design, don't worry about exact connections — plan the space and the wire routes.

Exact parts

Search the data sheet

  • DC gearbox motor — "TT motor"
  • L298N dual H-bridge motor driver
  • KY-023 control stick
  • SG90 9g micro servo
  • Freenove ESP32 WROOM
  • TCRT5000 infrared sensor (optional)

Plan your wiring

Leave room for these runs

  • Both motors → motor driver
  • Motor driver → breadboard
  • Servos → breadboard
  • Joystick (long wires) → breadboard
  • Battery pack → microcontroller
  • USB-C port accessible for code uploads

TIP: Try to hide wires for a better finish.

Safety guidelines

Hand tools & prototyping materials

Cutting

Blades and mats

  • Cut away from your body and keep hands clear of blades
  • Use cutting mats for X-Acto and utility knives
  • Foam core, clay, and cardboard are safe to prototype with — but still use blades carefully

In the lab

Dress and behave for the shop

  • Return all tools to their designated location after use
  • Wear safety glasses when cutting, soldering, or using power tools
  • Gloves only for hot materials or chemicals — never around rotating tools
  • Tie back long hair and avoid loose clothing or jewelry near machines
Policy

Review Nolop's safety policy here: nolop.org/safety

Hand making

What is foam core?

Light and rigid

A Styrofoam core laminated between two layers of smooth paper.

Better than cardboard

Easier to work with, and it produces neater, stronger models than corrugated cardboard.

Shape almost anything

Most geometric shapes can easily be created in foam core.

Foam core technique

Cutting: sharp blade, three passes

How to cut

Mat, ruler, fresh blade

  • Always cut on a cutting mat with a sharp X-Acto knife
  • Replace blades at the slightest hint of wear
  • Straight cuts are done with a metal ruler
  • Curves are cut carefully by hand

Hand making

Clay: organic forms

A naturally expressive, sculptural material — and the first step toward a 3D-printed part.

Why use clay?

Sculpt, then scan

  • A naturally expressive and sculptural medium
  • Easily manipulated by hand and simple tools
  • The model-making material of choice for complex, organic forms
  • Clay models can be scanned and refined in software for 3D printing
