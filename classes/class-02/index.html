The Robot Challenge

Design, build, and program a robot that moves as many balls as possible across the finish line in 5 minutes.

Scoring

  • 1 point per ball moved across the line
  • Maximum 10 balls per trip
  • Balls are dropped off in a standard container

The course

  • Move as many balls as possible in 5 minutes
  • Loading and drop-off zones are 10 feet (~3 meters) apart

Your robot

  • Designed, built, and programmed by your team
  • You cannot touch the balls or robot during the race
  • Robot and loading mechanism must fit in your team's project box
This is about more than winning. You'll experiment with ideas quickly, build foundational skills in sketching, prototyping, electronics, and coding, learn to collaborate in a team, and discover creative solutions to open-ended problems. At the end you'll have a robot to show off — and the maker skills for your final team project.

What's in your kit

Drive, support, and control — everything you need is in your team's kit.

Build notes: during early design you don't need exact connections — just allocate space to mount each component and route the wires. Search for data sheets online to verify dimensions, or measure the parts yourself. Tip: hide the wires for a better finish.

Hand making with foam core

Foam core is a Styrofoam core laminated between two smooth paper layers — lightweight but rigid, and easier to work with than corrugated cardboard.

You'll start with quick sketches, lay out paper templates, and cut on a mat with a sharp X-Acto knife — always in at least three passes: top paper, foam, then bottom paper. Straight cuts run along a metal ruler; curves are cut by hand.

Corners are where the craft shows: the butt joint, folded round, folded sharp, and rabbet. The rabbet hides the edge of the board and makes the strongest corner. For curves, cut the outside and add V-notches on the inside so the board bends smoothly.

Safety first. Cut away from your body and keep hands clear of blades. Wear safety glasses when cutting, soldering, or using power tools. Gloves only for hot materials or chemicals — never around rotating tools. Tie back long hair and avoid loose clothing or jewelry near machines. Read Nolop's safety policy at nolop.org/safety.

Clay & 3D scanning

For complex, organic forms, clay is faster than CAD — sculpt by hand, then digitize.

Clay is a naturally expressive, sculptural medium: easy to manipulate by hand and with simple tools, and the model-making material of choice for organic shapes. Start with a sketch, apply clay to a fixture, and sculpt the form.

Then scan the model with your phone using the Polycam app. Rotate and crop the scan, export it, and convert it to STL for 3D printing at convert3d.org/gltf-to-stl.
