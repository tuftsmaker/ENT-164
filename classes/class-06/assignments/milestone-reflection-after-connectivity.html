Purpose. Reflection is how making turns into learning: you stop, look at what you actually wired, asked for, and got running, and find out what it taught you.

This is a short written reflection on the digital making so far — the electronics, coding with AI, and going wireless. Answer these three prompts, a few sentences each:

  • Catch the surprise. Find a moment when the circuit, the code, or the AI didn't do what you pictured — a pin that stayed dark, a prompt that built the wrong thing, a page that wouldn't open. What did you notice, and what did you do next?
  • Think on your feet. Point to a decision you made mid-build without stopping to plan it — a wire you moved, a prompt you rewrote after reading the AI's answer. What were you reacting to?
  • Look back. Across everything so far — the electronics, the AI sessions, and going wireless: what would you keep exactly as it is, and what would you change — and why?

How to write it. Ground every answer in a specific moment — the wiring bench, the opencode chat, the first time your phone opened the page. A few honest paragraphs beat a polished essay.

Where to submit: go.tufts.edu/reflect, by the end of the day it is due. Reflections are part of your participation grade.
