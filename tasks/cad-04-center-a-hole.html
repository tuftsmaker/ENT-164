Task 4 · Laser-Ready File

Placing a Circle in the Center of a Rectangle

A 100 × 60 mm rectangle with one 10 mm diameter hole, exactly in the centre.

1:48 video One Onshape sketch Signed off by a TA
What to do What to hand in How it is checked
Watch first. Placing a Circle in the Center of a Rectangle (1:48)

What to do

Find the exact centre of the rectangle with construction lines, and put the hole there. Construction lines are guides — they are never cut.

Do first: Adding a Circle to Cut a Hole.

What to hand in

FileWhat it is
part.dxfrequiredexport after the construction lines are done — Onshape leaves them out of the DXF, which is the point of them
manifest.mdrequired

Put both files in one folder, run the checker, and upload the zip in Canvas. Ask opencode: check my cad-01 submission.

How it is checked

Every criterion below is checked on your file. The ones marked for a person are judged by a TA — they are never passed or failed by the checker.

Checked automatically

The two files are there

The file is in the submission.

Checked automatically

part.dxf opens as a DXF

The file is readable as a DXF and is not something renamed.

Checked automatically

Every path is closed

Every line end meets another line end. A corner that only looks joined would cut open.

Checked automatically

There are two closed shapes

The number of closed shapes is what the task asks for.

Checked automatically

Exactly one hole

Exactly 1 hole(s).

Checked automatically

The hole is 10 mm across

The hole measures 10 mm across.

Checked automatically

The hole is exactly in the centre

The hole is at the centre of the outline, not near it.

Checked automatically

No construction lines reached the file

No stray lines: guides are not exported, so a line in the file gets cut.

Checked automatically

It fits the laser bed

The part fits inside the laser bed (300 × 600 mm).

A person checks this

Onshape link recorded — a person opens it to see the construction lines

A TA opens your Onshape link to confirm the sketch is yours and committed.

Checked automatically

You ran the checker on this file

You ran the checker on this same file before submitting it.

Passing the checker is not the signoff. It means your file meets the written criteria. A TA reads it, checks anything marked for a person, and signs the task — then it counts toward the Laser-Ready File qualification.
