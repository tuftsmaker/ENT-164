Track · planned

Print-Ready Model

A 3D model that slices cleanly and prints without falling apart. The 3D counterpart to Laser-Ready File: CAD to STL to a sliced job the printer at Nolop accepts.

Not available yet 4 tasks planned Signed off by a TA
This track is not open yet. The tasks below are what it will contain, written down so the shape of it is clear. The videos have not been recorded and the checks have not been built, so nothing here can be submitted or signed off.

Why this track

Week 4 teaches the digital-to-physical workflow in three dimensions, and the class already asks students to design and print an object. Those submissions are currently judged by eye; this track makes the criteria explicit and checks what can be checked on the mesh.

What it will contain

Planned

Model a part from a sketch

(to record) sketch → pad/extrude in Onshape

Evidence: part.step or a share link; a screenshot of the model tree

Would check: units are mm; the body is a single solid; nominal dimensions match the task

Planned

Walls thick enough to survive

(to record) wall thickness, holes for a bolt, clearance

Evidence: part.stl

Would check: minimum wall ≥ 2 perimeters at a stated nozzle; hole diameters within tolerance

Planned

Export the STL and slice it

(to record) export from Onshape → PrusaSlicer → the settings class uses

Evidence: part.stl plus the .3mf/gcode, or screenshots of the slice

Would check: mesh is watertight and manifold; fits the printer envelope; flat face on the bed

Planned

Orient it so it prints

(to record) bridging, overhangs, why orientation is a decision

Evidence: the sliced file plus a one-line note on the chosen orientation

Would check: no unsupported overhang beyond the stated limit; the chosen face is on the bed

Then: Supervised print

Print one of your own models at Nolop with a TA or staff member present. The same shape as the laser track: the file being ready is one thing, running the machine is another.

What it needs first

Two things this track needs that the laser one did not: task videos (none of the existing tips cover 3D), and a mesh validator. `trimesh` handles watertightness and volume in pure Python; wall thickness needs sampling and is the hard check. Budget the validator as the real work, not the videos.
